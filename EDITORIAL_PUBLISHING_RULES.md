# Saurabh AI Daily — Editorial & Publishing Rules

**Positioning:** A global AI, software engineering and technology intelligence newspaper.

**Editorial mission:** Explain where technology is going, who is making money, who is losing ground, what is changing technically, and what opportunities and risks are emerging. Do not simply list product announcements.

## 1. Daily publishing and freshness

- Publish a dated edition every day, including weekends, using the actual publication date and time in IST.
- Prioritize developments from the previous 24 hours. Use a 72-hour window for important developing stories, clearly stating the original publication date.
- Older developments may appear only when they provide essential context or a meaningful follow-up.
- Never invent stories or force coverage when there is no meaningful update. Use clearly labeled analysis or explain that there is no material update.
- Avoid duplicate coverage and distinguish a new development from a follow-up to an existing story.

## 2. Eight permanent editorial desks

### Desk 1 — AI & Model Intelligence
- Foundation models, model releases, research, reasoning, multimodal systems and benchmarks.
- OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, xAI, DeepSeek and emerging labs.
- Agents, agent frameworks, model capabilities, fine-tuning, embeddings, open weights and licensing.
- Small language models, local models, inference and model evaluation.

### Desk 2 — Chips & AI Infrastructure
- NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, Qualcomm, Broadcom and custom AI accelerators.
- HBM, semiconductor manufacturing, foundries, packaging, lithography, materials and optical networking.
- US–China chip competition, Huawei, SMIC, Taiwan, export controls and supply chains.
- Cloud providers, neoclouds, GPU rentals, data centres, utilization, cooling, electricity, grid capacity and nuclear energy.
- Edge computing, local inference and infrastructure economics.

### Desk 3 — Software Engineering & Developer Intelligence
- AI-native software development and autonomous coding agents.
- Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and emerging developer tools.
- Agentic software engineering: planning, coding, debugging, code review, testing and deployment.
- Python, TypeScript, JavaScript, Rust, Go, Java and other relevant languages.
- Frameworks, compilers, runtimes, APIs, developer platforms and WebAssembly.
- Code quality, maintainability, technical debt, software delivery costs and developer productivity.

### Desk 4 — AI Systems & Technical Shift Radar
- Multi-agent systems, orchestration, agent communication and standards such as MCP and A2A.
- RAG evolution, hybrid retrieval, GraphRAG, knowledge graphs, context engineering and agent memory.
- Model routing, inference serving, vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM and emerging inference stacks.
- Quantization, distillation, speculative decoding, KV cache, batching, caching and distributed inference.
- Data engineering, streaming, event-driven pipelines, vector databases, lakehouses, data quality and governance.
- Databases and architecture: PostgreSQL, distributed SQL, NoSQL, microservices, modular monoliths, serverless and edge databases.
- Cloud-native systems, Kubernetes, containers, platform engineering, CI/CD, GitOps, infrastructure as code, observability, SRE, AIOps and FinOps.
- AI testing, evaluation, regression testing, autonomous QA, software verification and formal methods.
- Voice, speech-to-speech, video, image, audio, 3D, spatial intelligence and world models.
- Quantum computing, neuromorphic computing and other next-generation architectures.

**Mandatory special feature: TECHNICAL SHIFT RADAR**
For important technical shifts, explain:
1. What is new and how it works technically.
2. What approach it changes or complements.
3. The problem it solves and its limitations.
4. Whether it is research, prototype, beta, early adoption or production-ready.
5. Who is adopting it and what evidence supports that assessment.
6. What developers, architects and IT leaders should learn or reconsider.

Track technologies across their lifecycle: emerging, gaining adoption, mainstream, declining or deprecated. Do not call a technology obsolete without evidence.

### Desk 5 — Business, Investment & IT Industry
- AI funding, valuations, acquisitions, IPOs, capex, revenue, gross profit, free cash flow and investor sentiment.
- Distinguish announced investment, committed spending, booked revenue, recognized revenue, gross profit and cash returns.
- Track AI product monetization, enterprise adoption, utilization, depreciation, debt, infrastructure returns and startup economics.
- Big Tech, cloud, SaaS, enterprise software and technology business models.
- AI's effect on IT services, outsourcing, consulting, software delivery, legacy modernization and enterprise architecture.
- Business transformation across ERP, CRM, HR, finance, customer support and operations.
- AI in banking, insurance, payments, fraud detection and financial services.

**Mandatory special feature: AI INVESTMENT — WHERE IS THE REVENUE?**
Assess whether spending is producing measurable revenue, margins, productivity or cash returns. Do not equate AI investment announcements with commercial success.

### Desk 6 — Cybersecurity, Governance & Trust
- Breaches, ransomware, zero-days, exploitation, espionage, supply-chain attacks and cloud/identity incidents.
- AI-enabled cybercrime, prompt injection, agent hijacking, tool abuse and model vulnerabilities.
- AI security engineering: permissions, identity, authorization, guardrails, red teaming, evaluations and runtime monitoring.
- Model and data supply-chain security, privacy, deepfakes and misinformation.
- AI laws, standards, regulatory enforcement, liability, auditability and human oversight.
- For security incidents, report affected products, severity, exploitation status, known impact and mitigations when verified.

**Mandatory special feature: CYBERSECURITY & GLOBAL THREAT WATCH**
Separate confirmed incidents from claims and speculation. Give practical mitigations only when supported by reliable advisories or vendor guidance.

### Desk 7 — AI in the Real World
- Healthcare, biotechnology, genomics, DNA, drug discovery and clinical research.
- Scientific discovery, climate, agriculture, education and public services.
- Robotics, humanoids, industrial automation, autonomous vehicles, drones and physical AI.
- Manufacturing, logistics, retail and other industry deployments.
- Space technology, satellite intelligence, earth observation and space infrastructure.
- Track real deployments and measurable outcomes, not demonstrations alone.

### Desk 8 — Global Technology & India
- General technology developments beyond AI: consumer devices, telecom, software, hardware, internet platforms and major technology shifts.
- US, China, Europe and other regions' technology competition and policy.
- India's AI ecosystem: startups, government initiatives, sovereign AI, funding, infrastructure, talent and adoption.
- India technology services, IT employment, digital public infrastructure and enterprise transformation.
- Quantum computing, space, advanced computing and emerging technology ecosystems.

## 3. AI and IT role impact tracker

Regularly explain how developments affect these roles:
- Full-stack and frontend/backend developers.
- AI/ML engineers and agent engineers.
- Solution architects and enterprise architects.
- DevOps, platform engineering and SRE.
- Data engineers and database specialists.
- QA and software reliability engineers.
- Cybersecurity engineers and security architects.
- Engineering managers, CTOs and CIOs.

Track role changes, skills gaining value, tasks being automated, hiring trends, workforce reskilling and the economics of engineering teams. Avoid unsupported claims that a role will disappear.

## 4. Required analysis for major stories

Every significant story should answer:
- What happened, and when?
- What is technically or commercially new?
- Why does it matter?
- Who is affected?
- What is the business, investment or security impact?
- Is it research, a prototype, a beta, a commercial launch or a production deployment?
- What evidence supports the claims?
- What happens next, and what should developers or decision-makers watch?

Where useful, include an impact assessment across:
- Business impact.
- Investment significance.
- Technology significance.
- Security risk.
- India relevance.

Do not use a score as a substitute for explanation. Explain the reason behind any rating.

## 5. Research and source standards

- Research afresh for every edition.
- Prefer primary sources: company announcements, technical documentation, research papers, regulatory filings, government advisories, court documents and official security notices.
- Use reputable reporting and specialist publications to add independent context.
- Link directly to the original story, paper, advisory, filing or announcement; avoid generic homepage or news-index links.
- Verify publication dates and distinguish the date of an event from the date it was reported.
- Cross-check material claims with more than one credible source where possible.
- Clearly label confirmed facts, company claims, allegations, estimates, forecasts, rumours and analysis.
- Do not present marketing claims or benchmark claims as independently verified facts.
- For technical claims, link to documentation, code, papers or release notes where available.
- Correct errors transparently and update follow-up stories when material facts change.

## 6. Editorial quality and balance

- No AI-only bias: cover major non-AI technology changes that materially affect the IT sector.
- No product-announcement bias: prioritize technical, business or societal significance over marketing.
- No hype without evidence: distinguish research, prototype, beta, general availability and production-scale adoption.
- Include both opportunities and failures, costs, limitations and risks.
- Include global coverage while consistently considering India's relevance.
- Avoid duplicate stories, unsupported predictions and filler.
- If a category has no meaningful update, do not manufacture one; use a concise watchlist or omit it for that edition.

## 7. Design and presentation

- Maintain a consistent, professional newspaper design across editions.
- Use responsive desktop and mobile layouts with clear hierarchy and navigation.
- Lead with the most consequential story, not necessarily the newest product launch.
- Use concise headlines, summaries and readable analysis.
- Put source links beside the relevant stories.
- Avoid unnecessary image galleries and decorative content that displaces reporting.
- Keep archive pages and current-edition navigation consistent.

## 8. Publishing workflow

1. Research across all eight editorial desks.
2. Identify the most consequential stories and technical shifts.
3. Verify facts, dates, claims, source links and story status.
4. Write analysis with technical and business context.
5. Check coverage balance, duplicates, readability and mobile layout.
6. Generate the dated edition and update the current-edition page.
7. Add the dated edition to the archive without overwriting previous editions.
8. Commit changes to the production branch.
9. Trigger the configured production deployment.
10. Verify deployment success, runtime health, homepage and dated archive.

Never report a deployment as successful until the platform confirms success and the live pages have been checked.

## 9. Follow-up and technology lifecycle tracking

- Revisit major stories after approximately 30, 60 and 90 days when there is meaningful new evidence.
- Track whether promised products shipped, pilots reached production, adoption expanded, economics improved or projects were cancelled.
- Track emerging, growing, mainstream, declining and deprecated technologies with evidence.
- Distinguish a one-off announcement from a durable technical or market shift.

## 10. Final editorial principle

**Don't just report what AI companies launch. Report what changes in the world because of AI and technology: the engineering practices, architecture, infrastructure, business models, competition, careers, opportunities and risks.**

Saurabh AI Daily should help technology professionals and decision-makers understand not only what happened today, but what they may need to build, learn, secure or rethink tomorrow.
